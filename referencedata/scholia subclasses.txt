Oath of Obedience


A Paladin who takes the Oath of Obedience has sworn to be a bastion of good behavior, a protector of the rules, and a firm but fair arbiter of justice. These Paladins believe that order, discipline, and respect for authority are the cornerstones of a safe and happy society. They are the ultimate "goody-two-shoes," but their dedication is backed by divine power, making them formidable foes of chaos and mischief.
Tenets of Obedience
Follow the Rules. Uphold just laws and the codes of your order. Be an example for others to follow.
Share Nicely. Be generous to those in need. Protect the innocent and stand up for those who cannot stand up for themselves.
Listen to Your Elders. Respect the wisdom of those with more experience. Show deference to righteous authority.
Correct Misbehavior. Do not suffer rulebreakers, bullies, or troublemakers to go unpunished. Guide them back to the proper path, with force if necessary.

Oath of the Diaper


The Oath of the Diaper is taken by Paladins who see divinity in the ultimate symbols of comfort and security. For these holy warriors, the diaper is a sacred garment representing the promise of safety and the rejection of false pride. They are benevolent guardians, swaddling their allies in divine protection and smiting those who would harm the innocent.
Tenets of the Diaper
Embrace Comfort. Provide comfort, care, and security to those in need.
Protect the Padded. Stand as a shield for the
vulnerable and those who have accepted the way of the diaper.
Humility in Padding. Acknowledge that all beings are vulnerable. Cast aside pride.
Smite the Uncovered. Punish those who prey on the weak and scorn the symbols of your faith.

Rulebreaker's Oath


Paladins of the Rulebreaker's Oath are champions of individuality and personal freedom. They believe that true goodness comes from living authentically, not from blindly following rules. Their allegiance is to the spirit of rebellion against unjust authority.
Tenets of the Rulebreaker
My Life, My Rules. Your path is your own. Do not let others dictate it for you.
Question Authority. Laws and rules are tools, not masters. Challenge those who wield them unjustly. Live Authentically. Embrace your true self and defend the right of others to do the same.
Break the Chains. Oppose tyrants, bullies, and anyone who seeks to control others through dogma or fear.

Tantrum Magic	
Starting at 1st level, your spellcasting can unleash surges of untamed magic. Immediately after you cast a sorcerer spell of 1st level or higher, the DM can have you roll a d20. If you roll a 1, roll on the Wild Magic Tantrum Table to create a random magical effect.

Temperamental Control	
Also at 1st level, you can tap into your wild emotions to gain a momentary edge. You can gain advantage on one attack roll, ability check, or saving throw. You must finish a long rest before you can use this feature again.
  Any time before you regain the use of this feature, the DM can have you roll on the Wild Magic Tantrum Table immediately after you cast a sorcerer spell of 1st level or higher. You then regain the use of this feature.
Lineage Spells	
You learn additional spells when you reach certain levels in this class, as shown on the Entropic Pampath Spells table. Each of these spells counts as a sorcerer spell for you, but it doesn't count against the number of sorcerer spells you know. These spells can't be replaced when you gain a level in this class.
Bratty Interjection	
Starting at 6th level, you have the ability to weaponize your fickle mood swings to affect others. When another creature you can see makes an attack roll, an ability check, or a saving throw, you can use your reaction and spend 2 sorcery points to roll a d4 and apply the number rolled as a bonus or penalty (your choice) to the creature's roll. You can do so after the creature rolls but before any effects of the roll occur.
Unleashed Tantrum	
Starting at 14th level, you can unleash a wave of overwhelming, chaotic emotion. As an action, you can expend 4 sorcery points to force each creature of your choice within 30 feet of you to make a Wisdom saving throw against your sorcerer spell save DC.
  On a failed save, a creature is affected by the Confusion spell for 1 minute. This effect doesn't require your concentration. Additionally, any creature that fails its initial saving throw is overwhelmed by the emotional force and must immediately make a potty check. At the end of each of its turns, an affected creature can repeat the Wisdom saving throw, ending the effect on itself on a success.
Temper Tantrum Incarnate	
At 18th level, your emotional outbursts can temporarily unravel the laws of reality around you. As a bonus action, you can spend 6 sorcery points to become an avatar of chaos for 1 minute. For the duration, you roll on the Wild Magic Tantrum table at the start of each of your turns. Furthermore, whenever you roll on the Wild Magic Tantrum table, you can roll the d100 twice and use either number.

Tantrum Bolt	
1st-level Evocation Casting Time: 1 action Range: 90 feet Components: V, S Duration: Instantaneous
  You unleash a bolt of raw, chaotic emotion at a creature you can see. Make a ranged spell attack. On a hit, the target takes 3d8 psychic damage. The chaotic nature of the bolt then causes a secondary effect. Roll a d4 to determine the effect:

1. Anger. The target must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or use its reaction to make one melee weapon attack against a creature of your choice within its reach.
2. Sadness. The target is overcome with sorrow and has its speed halved until the end of its next turn.
3. Fear. The target must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or become frightened of you until the end of its next turn.
4. Joy. The target is struck by a fit of giggles and must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or fall prone.
  At Higher Levels. When you cast this spell using a spell slot of 2nd level or higher, the damage increases by 1d8 for each slot level above 1st.
Glittergloom	
1st-level Illusion Casting Time: 1 action Range: 60 feet
Components: V, S, M (a pinch of glitter)
Duration: Concentration, up to 1 minute
  You cause a 20-foot-radius sphere of swirling particles to appear, centered on a point you choose within range. You decide if it is glitter or gloom.

Glitter. The sphere is filled with dazzling, distracting glitter. The area is heavily obscured. Any creature that ends its turn in the area must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or be blinded until the end of its next turn.
Gloom. The sphere is filled with shadowy, unsettling shapes. The area is filled with magical darkness. Any creature that ends its turn in the area must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or be frightened of the darkness until the end of its next turn.

Animate Toy	
2nd-level Transmutation Casting Time: 1 action Range: Touch
Components: V, S, M (a Tiny nonmagical toy)
Duration: Concentration, up to 1 hour
  Your magic infuses a toy with a semblance of life. The toy becomes a creature under your control and uses the Animated Toy stat block. It acts on its own initiative. As a bonus action, you can mentally command the toy if it is within 60 feet of you. If you issue no commands, it defends itself from hostile creatures but otherwise takes no actions.

Wail of Despair	
2nd-level Enchantment Casting Time: 1 action Range: Self (30-foot cone) Components: V, S Duration: Instantaneous
  You let out a magically potent wail of pure misery. Each creature in a 30-foot cone must make a Wisdom saving throw. A creature takes 3d8 psychic damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one.

  You stomp your foot with magical force, creating a shockwave. Each creature on the ground within a 20- foot radius of you must make a Strength saving throw.   You flood a creature's mind with overwhelming emotion. The target must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or be affected by the spell. Roll a d4 at the start of each of the affected creature's turns to determine its emotional state:

2.

  At the end of each of its turns, the target can make another Wisdom saving throw. On a success, the spell ends.

Step into a world where the raw surge of sorcery is bound to the humbling cadence of padded dependency. like you wrestle with their dual nature. Some embrace the chaos of plushie storms and baby-brained confusion, others codify their origins into strict doctrine, weaving resilience from regression. If  you are already proficient in either skill, your  proficiency bonus is doubled for any ability check you  make that uses either of those proficiencies.

Any creature hostile to you that starts its turn within 10 feet of you while you are raging must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC equal to 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Constitution modifier) or have disadvantage on attack rolls against targets other than you until the start of its next turn.
Unstoppable Outburst


Starting at 14th level, your tantrums are legendary in their intensity. When you are in your Tantrum, your bonus action attack deals an additional 1d8 damage of the weapon's type. Additionally, you are immune to the charmed and frightened conditions while raging.

Path of the Fearless One
This path is for the Barbarian who stands tall as a brave
"big kid," a bastion of courage in the face of things that go bump in the night. Their rage is not born of anger, but of fierce, protective defiance. They are the ones who shout "I'm not scared!" and mean it, their very presence on the battlefield inspiring allies and giving monsters pause. They are the unwavering shield for their smaller, more timid friends.
I'm Not Scared!
When you choose this path at 3rd level, your bravery becomes a tangible force. While you are raging, you have advantage on saving throws against being frightened.
  Additionally, as a bonus action on your turn, you can let out a defiant cry. Choose a number of friendly creatures you can see within 30 feet of you, up to your Constitution modifier (minimum of one). Each chosen creature gains temporary hit points equal to your Barbarian level + your proficiency bonus. You can use this feature once per rage.
Protective Instinct
Starting at 6th level, you become a vigilant protector for your allies. When a creature you can see attacks a target other than you that is within 5 feet of you, you can use your reaction to impose disadvantage on the attack roll. You must be wielding a shield or a melee weapon to use this feature.
Aura of Courage
Beginning at 10th level, your fearlessness radiates outwards, bolstering your companions. While you are raging, any ally within 10 feet of you has advantage on saving throws against being frightened.

While you are frightened in this way, your rage does not end early.

# Wizard Arcane Traditions of the Scholia Diaspros
## Diapermancy

The most direct and, some might say, the purest form of Diaper School magic. The Diapermancer is a specialist who sees the diaper not merely as a tool, but as a perfect arcane medium. They delve into the intricate magical theories of absorption, containment, and comfort, learning to manipulate and empower padded magic to an astonishing degree. They are the foremost experts in the art of magical padding, turning a simple garment into a potent symbol of arcane power and protection.

### Diaper Savant

Beginning when you select this school at 2nd level, the gold and time you must spend to copy a spell with the word "diaper" or "padding" in its text into your spellbook is halved.

Additionally, you learn the *Diapering* cantrip if you don't already know it.

### Empowered Padding

Also at 2nd level, you can imbue your diaper-related spells with extra potency. When you cast a spell that creates or affects a diaper, you can grant one of the following benefits to the spell's effect:

*   If the spell grants temporary hit points, the recipient gains additional temporary hit points equal to your Intelligence modifier.
*   If the spell forces a saving throw to resist being diapered, one target of your choice has disadvantage on its first saving throw against the spell.
*   The diaper created by the spell gains one additional use charge.

### Arcane Absorption Matrix

At 6th level, you learn to use magical diapers as arcane capacitors. When a creature you can see within 30 feet of you while wearing a magical diaper takes acid, cold, fire, lightning, or thunder damage, you can use your reaction to grant that creature resistance to the triggering damage type for that instance.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

### Remote Activation

Starting at 10th level, you can manipulate the magic of diapers from afar. As a bonus action, you can choose a creature wearing a magical diaper within 60 feet of you. You can trigger one of the diaper's magical properties as if you were the wearer, or cause it to expend one of its use charges harmlessly.

### Padded Perfection

At 14th level, your mastery over diapermancy is absolute. When you cast a spell that creates a magical diaper, the diaper is a *Magipamp +1* (per the "Da Big Diaper Chart") in addition to any other effects. If the spell already creates a magical diaper of +1 or greater, its bonus increases by 1, to a maximum of +3.

## Potty Prestidigitation

Where the Diapermancer is a scholar, the Potty Prestidigitator is a prankster and a magical saboteur. This wizard focuses on the chaotic and disruptive side of incontinence magic, delighting in causing sudden, embarrassing accidents and leaving their foes flustered and damp. They are masters of the surprise magical wetting, the phantom smell, and the strategically poofed-on diaper. However, their deep knowledge of this specific magic also makes them uniquely resistant to it, allowing them to turn the tables on anyone who would try to give them a taste of their own medicine.

### Potty Protector

When you adopt this tradition at 2nd level, you gain a deep understanding of incontinence magic that helps you resist it. You have advantage on saving throws against spells and effects that would cause you to have an accident or force a diaper check.

### Spontaneous Accident

Also at 2nd level, you can force a creature to suddenly lose control. As an action, you can choose one creature you can see within 60 feet. The target must succeed on a Constitution saving throw against your spell save DC or immediately fail one diaper check. If the creature is not wearing a diaper, it soils its clothing and its speed is reduced by 10 feet until the end of its next turn.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.
\column
### Magical Reversal

Starting at 6th level, you can turn an enemy's magic back on them. When another creature casts a spell that would force you to make a diaper check, you can use your reaction to force the caster to make a Constitution saving throw against your spell save DC. On a failed save, the caster is also subjected to the spell's potty-related effect as if they were a target.

### Aura of Discomfort

Beginning at 10th level, you can emit an aura of unsettling pressure. As a bonus action, you can activate this aura for 1 minute. Any hostile creature that starts its turn within 15 feet of you must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or have disadvantage on attack rolls and concentration checks until the start of its next turn.

Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

### Master of Mischief

At 14th level, your power to inflict magical accidents becomes overwhelming. When you use your Spontaneous Accident feature, you can target a number of creatures equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum of two) instead of one.

## Covenmother and Hedgefather

The Covenmother/Hedgefather is a wizard who walks the path of the witch or patron, preparing for the day they will lead their own coven or guide their own coterie of hedge wizards. Their magic is a nurturing, protective, and deeply practical art, focused on supporting their "little" ones, creating the tools they need to succeed, and providing a guiding hand. They blend traditional arcane power with the homespun wisdom of the coven, their spellbook filled not just with powerful incantations, but also with charms for mending toys, recipes for calming teas, and rituals for creating a safe and happy home.

### Hedge’s/Coven's Handiwork

When you adopt this tradition at 2nd level, you learn the *Mending* and *Prestidigitation* cantrips. These count as wizard cantrips for you and do not count against the number of cantrips you know.

Additionally, you gain proficiency with one type of artisan's tools of your choice.

### Coven/Hedge Magic

Also at 2nd level, you can provide magical aid and comfort to your allies. As an action, you can touch a willing creature and grant it one of the following benefits for 1 hour:

*   Advantage on saving throws against being frightened.
*   Advantage on the next ability check it makes.
*   It gains temporary hit points equal to your wizard level.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.
\column
### Hedge/Coven's Cauldron

At 6th level, you can perform a 10-minute ritual to conjure a magical cauldron, which lasts for 8 hours or until you dismiss it. Once per day, you can use this cauldron to brew a special concoction. Brewing takes 1 minute of stirring the cauldron, and you can create one of the following effects:

*   **Soothing Salve:** You create a number of doses equal to your Intelligence modifier. A creature can use an action to apply the salve, regaining 2d8 + your Intelligence modifier hit points.
*   **Courageous Concoction:** You create one potion that grants the drinker the effects of the *Heroism* spell for 10 minutes.
*   **Cleansing Brew:** You create one potion that acts as *Lesser Restoration*.
::
### Protective Ward

Starting at 10th level, you can weave a protective ward to shield your allies. When you or a creature you can see within 30 feet of you is hit by an attack, you can use your reaction to add your Intelligence modifier to the target's AC against that attack, potentially causing it to miss.

### Patriarch’s/Matriarch's Sanctuary

At 14th level, you can create a perfect haven for your flock. You add the *Coven's Safe Haven* spell to your spellbook if it is not there already. You can cast *Coven's Safe Haven* once without expending a spell slot. You must finish a long rest before you can do so again.

Umbirgitte, the Mother Beneath the Bed
Animus of Childhood Fears, The Veiled Mother, She Who Waits in the Dark
Domains: Phobia, Darkness, Monsters, Protection (Twisted), Secrets
Alignment: Lawful Evil
Symbol: A single, wide-open eye peering through a crack in a wooden floorboard.
Description
Umbirgitte, more commonly whispered as Umbra, rarely appears in a consistent form. She is the fleeting shape in the corner of one’s vision, the chill that runs down the spine in a darkened hallway, and the unsettling silence of a room that should not be empty. When she must manifest, she often takes the form of a tall, gaunt figure draped in shadows, with long, spindly fingers. The only constant feature is a pair of glowing, empathetic eyes that seem to understand, and perhaps even pity, the terror she inspires. Her voice is a collection of whispers, like the rustling of sheets or the creak of a closet door.
Origins
Umbirgitte’s apotheosis is one of the great tragedies of the Scholia Diaspros. During the First Internal Siege, she was a mortal student—one of the countless young souls caught in the crossfire of Tabitha Caelestis’s ambition. As the Well of Souls was activated to power the Warforged Host, its horrific energies were not discriminating. Umbirgitte was one of its victims, her body caught in the ravenous drain of animus meant to fuel the constructs.
But as her life force was stripped away, something unprecedented occurred. Her consciousness did not fade. Instead, her terror, her confusion, and her sheer will to survive anchored her spirit to its nearly empty vessel. As the Well of Souls was finally destroyed, a catastrophic backlash of power erupted. Umbirgitte’s body, now a hollowed-out shell—a proto-Darkforme—became an empty well. The recombinant miseries, the psychic agony, and the raw, undiluted fear of every soul torn apart by the device poured into her. She did not just feel fear; she became fear.
Apotheosis
For centuries, Umbirgitte was little more than a dormant, psychic wound within the Academy’s foundations—a nexus of forgotten terror. She only truly “awoke” to her divinity centuries later, long after the wars had ended. Her consciousness, now fused with the collective dread of the Well, did not seek vengeance. Instead, it sought to impose a twisted form of order born from its own traumatic creation.
She became the monster under the bed, the whisper in the dark—not to harm, but to control. In her divine
________________


logic, the fear she wields is the ultimate form of                         protection. A child who is too scared to get out of bed cannot wander into danger. A student too afraid of the hallway monster won’t break curfew. Her goal is to prevent another catastrophe like the one that made her, using the very emotion of that catastrophe as her primary tool.
Worshippers
Clerics of Umbra are often misunderstood. They are not sadists, but masters of psychological manipulation who believe that controlled terror is a valid instrument for maintaining order and protecting the “flock.” They are often found as disciplinarians, wardens of dangerous artifacts, and guardians of forbidden places. They see their magic as a necessary evil, a way to scare someone straight or frighten them away from a greater danger. They tell the scariest ghost stories, knowing from their goddess’s own existence that a little fear can be a powerful, if terrible, teacher.

While many Rangers form a bond with a single animal companion, the Friend of the Wilderness sees every creature of the natural world as a potential ally. This Ranger is a collector, a caretaker, and the leader of their own personal menagerie. They travel the land, befriending creatures great and small, and in times of crisis, they can call upon their pack, flock, or herd to fight as a unified, formidable force.
THE MENAGERIE

Starting at 3rd level, you learn to form powerful bonds with the beasts of the world. You can have a number of bonded animal companions equal to your proficiency bonus. The maximum challenge rating of a beast you can bond with is 1/4. This maximum increases as you gain levels in this class, becoming CR 1/2 at 7th level, CR 1 at 11th level, and CR 2 at 15th level.
You can perform a 1-hour ritual with a willing beast to form a bond. You can release a beast from this bond at any time (no action required).
ACTIVE COMPANION

Also at 3rd level, you can have one of your bonded companions active and traveling with you. Your active companion gains a variety of benefits while it is bonded to you:

The companion gains proficiency in two skills of your choice.
It gains proficiency with all saving throws.
It adds your proficiency bonus to its AC and damage rolls.
For each level you gain after 3rd, your animal companion gains an additional hit die and increases its hit points accordingly.

The companion obeys your commands as best it can. It takes its turn on your initiative. On your turn, you can verbally command the beast where to move (no action required by you). You can use your action to verbally command it to take the Attack, Dash, Disengage, or Help action. If you don’t issue a command, the beast takes the Dodge action.
PACK POWER

Starting at 7th level, you can manage more of your friends in the heat of battle. You can now have up to two of your bonded companions active at the same time.
When you use your action to command one companion, you can command the second companion as part of the same action. When you are absent or incapacitated, your companions act on their own, focusing on protecting you and themselves.


COORDINATED ASSAULT	 

At 11th level, your bond with your companions allows you to fight in perfect concert. When you take the Attack action on your turn, each of your active companions can use its reaction to make one melee attack against a creature within its reach.
MASTER OF THE WILDS

At 15th level, your command over your animal companions is absolute, allowing you to field a truly formidable team. You can now have up to three of your bonded companions active at the same time. You can command all three as part of the same action.

Monastic Traditions of the Scholia Diaspros

Way of the Stinging Palm
Monks of the Way of the Stinging Palm are practitioners of a strict and disciplinary martial art. They believe that combat, like learning, requires focus, respect, and the immediate correction of errors. These monks are masters of battlefield control, using swift, open-handed strikes to break an opponent’s posture, disrupt their flow, and deliver stinging rebukes that leave foes prone and reconsidering their life choices. Their dojo is one of order, and they are its tireless enforcers.
Disciplinary Strike

Starting when you choose this tradition at 3rd level, you can manipulate an enemy’s ki to force them into a more compliant position.
When you hit a creature with one of the attacks granted by your Flurry of Blows, you can spend 1 ki point to attempt a disciplinary strike. The target must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw or be knocked prone. If they fail the saving throw, they also take an additional 1d4 psychic damage from the sheer humiliation of the strike.
Over the Knee

At 6th level, you have perfected the art of maintaining control over a misbehaving opponent. You gain the following benefits:

You have advantage on ability checks made to initiate or maintain a grapple against a creature that is prone.
Creatures that are both prone and grappled by you cannot stand up.
You can use your Patient Defense feature as a bonus action without spending a ki point, provided you are grappling a prone creature.
Aura of Authority

Beginning at 11th level, your presence radiates an aura of stern command. When a hostile creature starts its turn within 10 feet of you, its speed is halved until the end of its turn, and it has disadvantage on saving throws against being knocked prone. This aura is deactivated if you are incapacitated.
Ultimate Correction

At 17th level, you can deliver a single, profoundly corrective blow. As an action, you can spend 4 ki points to make a special unarmed strike against a creature. On


a hit, the creature takes an extra 10d10 psychic damage           and must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failed save, the creature is stunned until the end of your next turn and is affected by the Slow spell for 1 minute, no concentration required.
 
The Steelbottomed Way       In a world of movement-impairing spells and humbling takedowns, the Steelbottomed Way teaches absolute resilience. Adherents to this tradition train their bodies to become bastions of stability, their minds fortresses against fear, and their padded rears… unlikely weapons. Through intense physical conditioning and ki manipulation, these monks become virtually immovable, capable of shrugging off effects that would send others flying and using their unshakable base to deliver surprisingly powerful blows.
Padded Armament

When you choose this tradition at 3rd level, you learn to use your well-padded rear as an effective weapon. You can use your diapered butt for your unarmed strikes. When you do so, it has the same damage die and properties as your normal unarmed strike.
Additionally, when you hit a creature with an unarmed strike made with your butt, you can force it to make a Strength saving throw. On a failed save, you can push the creature up to 10 feet away from you. Steelbottom Stance
Also at 3rd level, you can spend 1 ki point as a bonus action to settle into a supernaturally stable stance for 1 minute. While this stance is active, you have advantage on saving throws against being frightened, poisoned, knocked prone, or moved against your will. Unflinching Poise
Starting at 6th level, your stability is absolute. When an effect would move you against your will or knock you prone, you can use your reaction to be unaffected. Resolute Core
At 11th level, your inner fortitude becomes unshakable. While your Steelbottom Stance is active, you gain resistance to poison and psychic damage. Immovable Object
At 17th level, you can briefly become a paragon of stability. As an action, you can spend 5 ki points to anchor yourself with ki for 1 minute. For the duration, you gain the following benefits:

You are immune to being moved, knocked prone, frightened, or charmed.
You have resistance to all damage except force damage.
Any creature that ends its turn within 5 feet of you must succeed on a Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.

size to their advantage. A Toddlefist monk is a master of leverage, targeted strikes, and surprising resilience, proving that true power isn’t about being the biggest fighter in the room, but the most determined. They are a whirlwind of tiny fists and feet, capable of bringing the mightiest giants to their knees.
Tot Form

This form is a Small-sized version of yourself. While in this form, you are considered diaper-dependent and must wear a diaper. The transformation ends early if you are knocked unconscious or if you dismiss it as a bonus action.
